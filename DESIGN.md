# Trackbench — Design Context (name chosen 2026-11-20; it was Tallysmith until real people could not tell what that was)

*Read at the start of every session. Terse on purpose: current decisions, not history. History and the "why" for a human reader live in CASE_STUDY.md. Last updated 2026-11-16.*

## What it is

A tracker you architect yourself. Each person builds their own ruleset: what they log, how it's scored (or not scored), how hard it pushes or punishes, and how loud the experience feels, from a quiet checklist to a full game. The ruleset is the product. Others can read, copy or join someone's ruleset, so a ruleset works like a shareable "build" in a game.

Not a coach tool and not a dungeon master who sets your goals. The user is the designer of their own motivation system.

## Stance (decided 2026-11-16)

- Go in believing it can work, as a business or as a portfolio piece. Both outcomes count.
- Scope is **not** deliberately small. Plot's MVP took about a week and polish took about three months; the same pace applies here. Aim for more than a traditional MVP.
- Stop conditions are allowed as checkpoints but are **not** a guiding principle.
- Current phase: free-form design discussion. No code yet. Chatting is cheap; keep pushing ideas before building.
- Monetization is designed in from the start, not added at the end, because it can be foundational.

## Locked decisions

1. **No AI in the product.** Dropped 2026-11-16. Onboarding is a guided "what interests you?" flow (character-creation style) where people opt into the features they want; the product then assembles their interface and rules. Everything is editable later. Customization depth is the hook.
2. **A ruleset is a data format**, defined cleanly from day one. Onboarding is one program that fills it in; the editor is another way to change it. (If AI is ever reconsidered, it would be a second filler of the same format.)
3. **Rules and world are separate layers.** Rules: what gets logged, what counts, what changes. World: how it looks and feels (plain list, RPG character, garden, run, nothing). Points are one option, not the foundation. "No points at all" and "tons of points" are both valid.
4. **Randomness is optional.** Some people want variable rewards, others want predictable effort-to-reward. A dial, not a mode.
5. **Daily use is one tap.** Setup can be deep; logging must stay fast whatever the ruleset. Origin: Nick's own gym tracker works because it's barebones and quick.
6. **Visual blocks** for editing rules. **Quiet vs game intensity is the user's choice.**
7. **Sharing is always free and never monetized.** Two forms: copyable template, and joining a friend's world. Sharing a system is also content ("how does their setup work?"), like game build culture.
8. **First testers: friends and peers, and Nick himself** (gym lifts, nutrition, weight).
9. **Mobile app from the start; no website.** Decided 2026-11-16. Android friends test first; pay for Apple's developer account when iOS testing is needed. Development testing can stream to Nick's phone through the Expo dev server.
10. **Sharing runs through a friend system and shareable links.** A link sent outside the app asks for install if needed, then opens in the app to add the friend or join their setup. A code stays as a fallback. A link needs a tiny redirect host, but there is still no real website. (Revised 2026-11-16; replaces "in-app code only".)
11. **Opt-in modules over a small tracking core** (see Architecture). Users choose the elements they want directly. We do not sort people into player types with a quiz.
12. **Build order: a barebones tracker first, then add options one at a time.** The event system exists from day one even with no modules on it, so modules can plug in later without rework.
13. **Onboarding explains itself.** Presets for common types of people, plus guided questions. Either way the result ends on a readable list of every setting those choices produced, each editable.
14. **Social is an opt-in module**, never required: friends, joining setups, real-world stakes between people.
15. **Gamification comes from systems, not game art.** Game-style presentation turns some people off (it's why Cura moved away from a Habitica-like look). Every person gets a unique "front" assembled from their own setup (see Architecture).
16. **Solo-first, with a backend planned.** (Corrected 2026-11-17: this was written as "offline-first", which was a misreading of "solo first".) The app is built to work for one person first, with accounts, cross-device sync and the social features on a backend (Supabase is the likely choice) layered on top. Logging should still feel instant, so the screen never waits on the network to record an entry, but nothing is designed around staying offline. A name is asked for at the start because accounts will need it.
17. **Stack: Expo (React Native) with TypeScript.** One codebase for Android, iPhone and a web preview used for development. Local storage via AsyncStorage for now.
18. **Multi-tracker is the foundation, but not the pitch.** Direction set 2026-11-16 after the gym set-list case. The "track anything" builder category is crowded; what none of the apps found offered is a motivation system (streaks, rules, game elements, notifications) designed once and applied across all your trackers, plus shareable setups and a unique front. Pitch: the system over everything you track. Presets and templates (a fast gym tracker, a food log) keep each tracker as quick as a specialist app.
19. **Two levels: trackers feed a system.** Decided 2026-11-16. Trackers are customized per job; the *system* is the shared layer (state, rules, front) a person designs across them. Each tracker can be part of the system or kept standalone with a toggle. The data model supports several systems from the start (a "world" is a system), with each tracker belonging to one or none; the UI starts with a single system and exposes more later.
20. **Onboarding order.** (1) Pick the things you want to track, each offered as a preset with a plain explanation (reps and weight, study blocks, ...). (2) Then offer how to tie them together: streaks, game elements, notifications. (3) End on the readable list of resulting settings. Minimum required is one tracker; system questions have sensible defaults and stay reachable later. Aim to get the first real log in early, because research shows setup-heavy flows lose people. More leeway than a typical app is fine since customization is the pitch.
21. **Modules use slots.** Modules in the same slot are mutually exclusive (for example, how misses are handled: strict streak, forgiving score, or nothing). Modules in different slots stack. Every change to shared state is written to a visible ledger with the rule that caused it ("why did I lose 5 points?").
22. **Monetization direction (hypothesis).** Free: generous, unlimited basic trackers and one system, so the customization pitch is fully felt. Paid: extra systems, advanced modules and the raw rule editor, extra field types, themes. Avoid a hard cap on the number of trackers, which competitors use and users dislike.
23. (Superseded by 25.) **Types are generic behaviors, not subjects.** Direction set 2026-11-16 after reading Nick's Fitness Tracker. A type is named for what it does (for example Sets, Checklist, Measure, Count, Check, Timer), never for a subject like "Lifting". Presets are ready-made setups whose supporting text says what each is good for ("works well for reps and weight, or study blocks"). A type can serve many subjects, and one thing can use more than one type. The prototype's subject-named presets (Lifting, Food and macros) are to be replaced. Structure confirmed in decision 24: a tracker holds several typed sections.
24. (Partly superseded by 25: the workspace and home overview stand; sections inside a tracker and in-tracker settings do not.) **A tracker is a workspace; home is the overview.** Decided 2026-11-16. Adding a tracker opens something like Nick's own Fitness Tracker inside the app: a full space with its own sections (each section has a generic type), plan, logging, history, targets and settings. The home screen is an overview of all trackers (summary, progress, streak), not a list of one-line things. Depth and customization live inside each tracker. Light trackers (water, a habit) can still be logged from their home card. Presets become tracker templates: a starting set of sections plus a sample plan, with supporting text saying what it is good for. Every tracker also gets the universal features: day strip and history, plan editor with versions, targets, schedule and rest days, text export and import, and the system toggle. The test for the model: can it reproduce Nick's Fitness Tracker from configuration alone.
25. **One universal tracker with a toolbox; no fixed types.** Decided 2026-11-16, correcting 23 and 24. Nutrition is its own tracker, separate from lifting; a tracker has no internal sections. Every tracker has the same tools and differs only in which are switched on and how many entry fields it has, from one tap to Nick's full Fitness Tracker. Presets (a fitness tracker, a nutrition tracker, study) are just starting configurations with supporting text. Customization lives on the tracker; the front and system interpret each tracker through its measures. **Use and build are separate:** the tracker's daily screen stays lean and fast, and its configuration lives in a separate builder reached from the tracker, never as a tab inside it.
    The toolbox (the ceiling): (1) entry fields, zero to many (number, text, choice, check, duration, with units, steppers, defaults); (2) entry style, free entries or planned slots; (3) plan, with slots, targets, per-weekday schedule, rest days and versions with effective dates; (4) groups, items with values and variants (meals, exercises); (5) targets on measures, daily or weekly, with progress; (6) previous values shown and auto-filled; (7) derived measures from a fixed menu (sum, max, latest, average over N days, weekly total, change versus previous); (8) history, with a day strip, calendar and past days locked or editable; (9) text export and import; (10) the system link, meaning which measures feed the front and what counts as success.
    **Surfacing depth (direction, 2026-11-16):** most people lean on presets, and depth must never jumpscare anyone. Each tracker's builder shows 3 to 5 main switches in plain language up front (for example plan my week, show previous numbers, daily target, rest days). Each tool has a collapsed "more options" group that summarizes its current state in one line. Advanced items (derived measures, plan versions, export and import, system link) sit last. Tools a tracker does not use appear as quiet "Add ..." tiles with one-line explanations, so depth is discoverable without being loud. A tracker's setup receipt lists what is on, like onboarding's.
    **Units (decided 2026-11-16):** the default unit system (imperial or metric) is an app-level setting in the System tab. A unit field follows that default, and any single field on any tracker can override it. Values are stored in base units (kg, km, cm, ml) so changing units never corrupts history; only the display and entry convert. Fixed units such as kcal, g and min are not converted.
    **Data transfer (decided 2026-11-16, extends tool 9):** three things, all plain text. (1) *Export data*, readable by an AI, with logs, plan and previous-week comparison. (2) *Import data*, a machine-readable backup that merges entries by id. (3) *Export and import structure*, a tracker's configuration as validated JSON, so an AI can edit a plan and it can be imported back; this is also the sharing format, replacing the earlier "code" idea.
    **Depth ceiling:** no free-form formulas, conditionals, loops or relations between trackers inside a tracker. Cross-tracker logic exists only in the system layer, reading measures. A tracker stores and measures; the system interprets. New trackers start at the simplest configuration and depth is opt-in.

26. **Setup is a guided flow of bite-sized steps, not a menu.** Decided 2026-11-16 after the v3 builder tested as confusing and heavy. Principle: break a long menu into short steps, each asking one plain question about the thing being set up, and make clear how each answer shapes that thing (a small live preview of the tracker stays on screen and updates as you answer). Every step has a default already chosen so "Next" always works. Adding a tracker is its own onboarding, and editing a tracker reopens the same flow with current answers; there is no separate builder screen. Supersedes the main-switches-and-collapsed-groups builder in decision 25's "surfacing depth".
    Flow: (1) pick a starting point, (2) what you record each time, shown as plain chips, with units from the default and a tap to change one, (3) how you log, whenever you do it or following a plan, (4) the plan, only if chosen, (5) any goal, (6) a final list of everything chosen with "Add to my system" and "More options". Simple trackers skip steps (a counter is about three screens). Targets, averages, locking past days, success rules and per-field unit overrides live under one "More options" with defaults set by the earlier answers.
    Plan step without AI: the default is *build it as I go* (log a first session normally, then "save as my plan"; the exercises and set counts actually done become the plan; meals work the same with "repeat every day"). Alternatives: start from an example and edit, or paste a structure (for people who generate one with an AI). Typed plain-text plans are not the main path: Nick does not set his own plans up that way.
    Open before building: whether the live preview helps or adds noise, and whether steps 3 and 5 merge for simple trackers.

27. **Home is the hub; no Front tab, no System tab.** Decided 2026-11-16. Home is both the overview and how you reach each tracker: a summary of your system at the top (points, level, streaks if on), then the trackers, then "Add tracker". Tapping the summary opens a **Progress** page (what the Front showed: per-tracker streaks and 14-day strips, points, hearts, and "why did my score change?"), with "Edit how it works" opening the system as a guided flow like adding a tracker (intensity, streaks, game elements, rules, week preview). **Settings** opens from a gear on Home: appearance, units, data (export, import, backup) and developer tools. No bottom tab bar. The word "Front" is retired; a shareable identity view can come back later as a Share action on Progress.
    **UI customization (density and corner settings removed for now, one fixed spacing and soft corners while the core is tuned):** theme (a few full looks plus light, dark, follow the phone), accent color, home layout (rows or cards); candidates: text size and a color per tracker. Making it live means a theme layer that every screen reads. Also to do: clean up the visuals (fewer boxes, one clear hierarchy per screen, a consistent spacing and type scale, proper icons with emoji optional) and rethink the button system (see open questions).
28. **Goals are repeating or long-term, and they are separate.** Decided 2026-11-16. A repeating goal is a kind (show up: log N times; hit an amount: at least, at most or within a range; finish the plan), over a window (day now; week and month planned). A tracker can have several, including finishing the plan, and a day is good only when all are met (success rule `targets`, with an optional plan percentage). No goal is the default. Long-term targets (reach a value by a date) are a separate feature with a progress line and pace; they do not feed streaks or points the way repeating goals do (they may award points when reached). The goal screen lists every choice as a selectable card (chosen ones outlined and showing their setting; no goal is chosen when nothing else is) and states that long-term targets are set up separately. Built: daily goals. Planned: weekly and monthly windows, then long-term targets.
    Open: what exactly is wrong with the current buttons (placement, size, style, or how many kinds there are).

## Core model (draft)

- **Things you log** (inputs).
- **Rules**: when something happens over a period, change something.
- **State** that rules change (points, health, streak, level, whatever the ruleset defines).
- **Time**: daily and weekly cycles, resets.
- **Lapse rules** as a first-class block: comebacks, pauses, expiring rules. Research says apps should plan for people stopping.
- **World / presentation**: the visual and emotional layer.
- **Simulator**: preview what a ruleset would do ("miss 3 days, what happens?") before committing. Reuses the approach from the earlier Loot Game sims.

## Architecture (draft, 2026-11-16)

Three layers. Each is separate so people can change one without touching the others.

**1. Core (always on, minimal).** Trackers, entries and time.
- A *tracker* is something you log, and a person can have many different kinds at once (lifts, food, weight, habits). A tracker is a set of typed *fields* (number, check, duration, choice, text), optionally with a repeating group of fields (a set-list is a repeating group of weight and reps). Check, count and number are just presets of this. A tracker also has a schedule (daily, chosen weekdays, N times per week) and an optional target.
- Each tracker defines named *measures* computed from its entries (a lift's volume, a day's calories, "done today"). Modules and rules work on measures, never on raw fields. That is the contract between trackers and modules, and it is what lets one rule span different trackers ("protein target hit and a workout logged").
- Every logged entry and every period close (end of day, end of week) emits an *event*.
- Entries are stored separately from the ruleset. That lets rules change without losing history, and lets a new ruleset be replayed over past entries (the simulator and backtesting).

**2. Modules (opt-in during onboarding, add or remove later).** Each module declares which events it listens to, what state it owns, which rules it brings (with adjustable dials) and which screens it adds. Every module ships with a quiet default. Candidates: Points; Streaks (strict, grace-day, or a Loop-style score that goes up with done days and down with misses); Health and penalties; Levels and XP; Loot and randomness; Quests and weekly challenges; Reflection; Social (share code, join a world). Later: stakes.

**3. Front (presentation only).** Each enabled module contributes tiles to a person's front, a showcase of their system and results that friends can see. Someone running levels shows a level, a lifter shows PRs, a person with no points shows consistency over time. Because everyone enables different modules, every front looks different. Game-style themes and art are optional extras, not the main idea. Visibility to friends is controlled per tile. (Replaces the earlier "world" layer.)

**Rule engine.** A rule is a trigger (an event) plus a condition plus an effect on state. Rules run in a fixed order, one pass per event. Effects change state only; they never emit new events. This rules out the loops and conflicts research finds non-programmers produce in trigger-action systems.

**Three levels of control (progressive disclosure).** 1) Pick modules. 2) Adjust each module's dials. 3) Edit raw rules in an advanced editor, with a plain-language explanation and the simulator ("this would fire 12 times this week") to catch mistakes.

**One document.** A ruleset is a single versioned document (trackers, enabled modules, dial values, custom rules, world). The share code is that document encoded. Importing validates the version.

**Systems and balance.** A system is a container: the trackers assigned to it, its enabled modules, dial values and rules. Gamification balance is set with a few dials (reward size, penalty size, forgiveness) offered as presets like Gentle, Standard and Hardcore, not raw numbers. The simulator shows what a typical week and a bad week would do before the person commits. Underneath it stays complex; on the surface each choice is one clear question.

## Monetization (foundational; open)

Constraints already fixed: sharing is free forever; do not paywall editing your own system.
Direction (see decision 22): free is generous; paid adds extra systems, advanced modules and the rule editor, extra field types and themes. Other candidates: cross-device sync and backup. A later shop where people sell setups for commission is possible but not near-term.
Benchmarks: Finch charges roughly $40–70 per year (third-party estimate); Streaks and Loop show the low-priced end. Notion template sellers show people do pay for shared systems.
**Open question:** the earlier idea of charging per AI-created world is gone with the AI decision. Needs a new core reason to pay. Decide early, revisit as the design firms up.

## Research takeaways (sources in CASE_STUDY.md)

- Trackers lose people to effort without payoff, novelty fade (about 3–8 weeks), and one missed day ending everything. Fixed punishment backfires for some. Evidence is mostly vendor blogs; the Epstein UW papers are the solid source.
- Simplicity wins in reviews (Streaks, Loop). Game layers split users by type; the issue is how much of the app they take over.
- Heavy setup hurts: too many options means people configure instead of doing. Start with a few things, end onboarding on a first real check-in.
- Notion-style template sharing does make money for creators, but sales depend on the creator's audience.

## Setup flow steps

A starting card already answers what is recorded and how, so a card goes Start, Name, then Summary; Counter and Time blocks add a Goal screen because a target is their point. Only "Start from scratch" asks Record, Log (numbers only), Plan and Goal. Editing opens straight on the summary. Anything on the summary is one tap from its screen and Done returns there (the Name header, You record, You log, Plan, Goal). The Plan screen is one screen: days it is on (a card sample uses Mon, Wed, Fri), same list every day or different each day, and start with a sample or empty. Name is empty and required with an icon picker (search and categories; no preview card on any step). No skip-to-end button. Choice cards use `Option` from `ui/kit`: bold headline, short subhead, icon, radio mark, fixed border width.

## Goals

Goals are quests with visible progress, and they are what drives the system. Each tracker has a **daily goal** from setup (its "good day": show up by default, or amounts, a plan, or staying clean) and any number of **goals** added later on the tracker page (`Tracker.goals`, `core/goals.ts`). A goal counts something you record: a field (Weight, Minutes, Mood), the number of entries, or the days you logged anything. It combines it as a total, highest, lowest, average or latest (`GoalDef.field` and `agg`), over a calendar week, a calendar month or all time, aims at least, at most, within a range or to reach a number, and may have a deadline. Goals never use special measures like volume or top weight; those are just a field combined a way (top weight is Weight, highest). Goals saved earlier with a measure still work. Progress is always computed from entries (`goalProgress`), shown as a thick bar with the numbers, what is left, days left and whether you are on pace, green when met and red when over (`ui/GoalBar.tsx`). Where it shows: a Goals row under the week strip, a full Goals screen (daily goals, active goals, finished goals, and an Add a goal builder), and a Goals card at the top of Home. Weeks begin on Monday or Sunday (Settings). **Goal presets** (`core/goalPresets.ts`): Add a goal opens a short list of suggestions built from the tracker's own shape, fields and history (show up a few days a week, finish every planned set, a weekly total scaled from a typical entry or the daily target, move a reading a small step in eight weeks, beat your best, an average rating, no slips this week or month or for 30 days); each has one line saying why it is a good goal, tapping one opens the builder pre-filled, and every part can be changed before saving. Like tracker templates they are configuration, never keyed to what the tracker was started from. A limit or range goal over your own dates is judged at its end date, not on the first day. **System:** daily goals decide good days as before; finished weeks, finished months and one-time goals reached produce events that the engine turns into points (3, 8 and 10 times the base reward); a miss costs nothing. The first partial week or month is not judged.

**Anatomy of a tracker.** A tracker is made of three kinds of parts, and each one can be reached from both setup and the tracker page: what you **record** (fields, set in setup), its **items** (the rows of a plan, like exercises, edited inline on the tracker page), and **goals** over either (set on the Goals screen). Goals can count a field across the whole tracker or aim at one item (`GoalDef.item`, matched by name because plan slots come and go; "Bench press: highest weight at least 100 kg"). The setup summary has a "Goals over time" line that opens the Goals screen, so setup and goals read as one system. Not yet: adding a new field straight from the tracker page (it still goes through setup), and a goal shortcut on each plan row.

**Goal screen layout.** Adding or changing a goal takes the whole screen: the goal's sentence sits at the top of the builder and updates as you choose, and nothing else is listed above it. Suggestions use the same choice cards as setup, with a one-line reason each. Suggestion values come from the tracker's real data where there is some (last reading, each item's own best), and from the field's default otherwise.

**Neutral by default (rule).** Nothing a person sees may assume a domain unless they or their starting point chose it. A planned tracker's words default to Item, Round and Target (`core/terms.ts`); the gym start and gym templates choose Exercise, Set and Range as plain configuration, and anyone can reword them under "Change the wording". Goals, the plan editor, exports and the "Rounds done" measure all read the tracker's own words. The field menu (weight, reps, calories and so on) is a menu of kinds of number, not behaviour. What stays internal and invisible: the measure id `sets` and the `Slot.sets` property (renaming would break saved data; users never see them). Trackers saved before this keep Exercise/Set/Range on load.

**Same bones (rule, enforced by `sameBones.test.ts`).** There is no unique preset. `core/presets.ts` holds only labels, a default name and icon, and optional sample plans; it has no tracker structure. Every starting point, template and shape is a set of answers built by the same step as "Start from scratch". Tests check that (1) a starting point's identity changes nothing about the structure it builds, (2) every template and shape survives a round trip through the builder unchanged, and (3) the hand-built reference trackers (`fixtures/nick.ts`) can be made with the builder, compared by meaning. Known small gap: which measure is the tracker's headline number (`primaryMeasure`) is picked by the builder (the combined number if any, else the first number), not chosen by the person. Trackers saved by earlier versions are upgraded on load with the explicit settings new ones carry (wording, readings).

**Choosing controls (rule).** Anything that picks one thing from a list uses the shared `Dropdown` (`ui/Dropdown.tsx`): a firm 2px bordered field on a raised background with a chevron button, an open panel with its own border, row dividers, a check on the chosen row, a search box past eight rows, a scroll limit, and space after the last row. Spacing and border values live in `SEPARATION` (`constants/layout.ts`), so tuning is one change. Other pickers (the calendar, chip groups) should move to these constants as they are touched.

**Buttons, not links (rule).** Anything tappable that is not a row or a card looks like a button: a border, a raised background, an icon where it helps. No floating coloured text. `Button` is the full-width one, `SmallButton` the compact one (it can be look-only inside a card that is already tappable, like "See all"), and `BackButton` is the one back control on every screen (arrow and where it goes). Still text and to be revisited: the week-strip arrows and the value readouts that use the accent colour.

**Navigation (rule).** There is no hub. Every tracker is a place of its own, reached from a bar along the bottom (`ui/TrackerBar.tsx`) that lists all trackers and ends with "New". Sizing follows the phone width (`itemWidthFor`, `constants/layout.ts`): it aims for five items across (fewer on a narrow phone, never below a minimum width); with fewer trackers they share the width; with more, the row scrolls one item at a time (snapping) and an arrow shows in a margin that is always reserved on each side (so it never covers an item) when more is out of sight that way. Names wrap to two lines instead of being cut off. Each tracker shows whether you have logged today: a green tick when its daily goal is met, a dot when something is logged. Press and hold a tracker to lift it: drag left or right to move it (it stays where you drop it), and Edit and Delete appear above the bar (delete asks twice). The app opens on the first tracker you have not finished today. Top right of every tracker page: Progress (showing your points when there is a system) and Settings, both system-wide. Progress holds the all-goals overview and, later, social. Goals, setup and flows hide the bar and have a pinned back button. Open question: whether Progress or a future social page ever earns a spot in the bottom row; for now the bottom row is only what you track.


**Which items a goal suggestion picks.** For a planned tracker, up to two items: those with a number logged most in the last eight weeks, then the most recently logged. Items whose number never rises above zero are skipped. Each says how many entries it is based on.

**Goal builder.** The goal sentence is pinned at the top while the options scroll beneath it. "Which item" is a collapsed row showing the current choice; it opens to chips, with a search box once there are more than eight.

**One goal system.** Every goal is a goal, and "each day" is just one way a goal repeats (Each day, Each week, Each month, or your own dates). A daily amount goal is stored as one of the tracker's daily targets because the daily rule, streaks and points read those; `dailyGoals`, `withDailyGoal` and `withoutDailyGoal` (`core/goals.ts`) convert between the two, so the Goals screen lists and edits them like any other goal. The daily rules that are not an amount (show up, finish the plan, stay clean) show as one card that opens setup. Each day is offered only for things that have a daily measure (a number field, the count, a combined number), never for "days logged". Adding a daily goal to a "show up" tracker replaces the show-up rule.

**Adding a goal.** Suggestions are cards with two buttons: Add now (saves it as it is) and Change first (opens the builder). The builder folds "what to count", "use the", "over" and "your goal is" under one "Measured as" line with a "Change how it's measured" button when it starts from a suggestion or an existing goal; from scratch everything is open. Any tracker with choice fields can count only entries where a choice has a given option ("Only count entries where"), stored as `GoalDef.where`. Time fields get a steady-window suggestion; choice fields a days-per-week suggestion. Finished goals have a Repeat button (same length, from today). While setting up a new tracker the same goals are made in one section (see below).

## Calendar and units

The tracker page keeps a Monday-first seven-day strip; tapping its week label opens a month grid with each day's result, to jump to any date. A tracker can use its own units (my default, imperial or metric) from the Units line on its summary; goal numbers convert with it.

## Shapes and templates

**Rule: a starting point is only configuration.** Nothing in the code may depend on which starting point a tracker came from, or on what a field is called. Anything a template does, a person can build from scratch with the same general options, and `scratch.test.ts` checks it (gym plan, meal plan, weigh-in, mood, sleep, water, check-in, avoiding, study all rebuilt from a blank tracker and compared structurally). The general options: fields of any type, a field marked "a reading" (shows the latest value for a day) or adding up, a combined number built from two fields (weight times reps, called anything), planned items that are filled in each time or ticked with their own numbers (`planStyle`), daily or avoid mode, goals, wording, units. Volume and top weight are no longer built in: volume is a combined number the gym template adds explicitly, and top weight is a goal on Weight, highest.

Two layers. **Shapes** are the few neutral ways a tracker can work (check-in, avoid something, counter, daily value, log, planned list); they are no longer a screen, since the record screen asks the same question, but they remain in the code as the neutral baseline that the same-bones tests check. **Templates** (`core/templates.ts`, 40 across Fitness, Body, Health, Mind, Habits, Money, Study and Life) are finished starting points: fields, wording, goals, schedule and an empty plan. The start screen is a heading, a search box, Build your own, then Popular (the top 8 in a guessed popularity order) and every other template under a heading for its category; templates you already have say Added. Searching flattens it to one list and always ends with "Build <word> your own way", which opens Build your own with the name typed. Every new tracker opens on the same summary: a template fills it in, Build your own leaves it empty (You log reads Nothing yet, and Start logging opens You log until something is added). Each row is one tap from the summary.

Daily habits is a list of habits ticked each day (the Medication shape, the item called Habit, starting empty); one tap per tracker is the Check-in shape, used by the avoid presets, Bills and Plants. Reminders, streaks, charts and rewards for habits come from the shared systems, not from this preset.

Tools can switch a number to be filled from the phone: "Steps from my phone" appears when a tracker has a number called Steps (on for the Steps preset) and is stored as the tracker's source for that field.

Saving: the app holds its data in memory and saves changes to the phone's SQLite database a row at a time (`core/persist.ts`); trackers, entries and settings mirror the cloud tables in `docs/supabase-schema.sql`. Cloud backup (`core/sync.ts`, `core/cloud.ts`) is off until switched on, signs in anonymously, sends rows marked unsent, then takes rows changed in the cloud since it last asked; the later change to a row wins. Settings > Developer holds the switch for now.

Accounts: everyone has one and it is the first screen (core/auth.ts, screens/AuthScreen.tsx): Google first, or email and password, then the name screen asks for a name (prefilled from Google; no unique usernames, people are linked by codes). Backing up is on whenever signed in; sign out clears the phone after sending what is waiting; deleting the account removes the cloud data and the phone's. Only in testing can it be skipped.

**Field types** (`ui/FieldInput.tsx`): number, note, rating (tap 1 to 5 or 1 to 10, shown as a trend), choice (pick from your own list) and time of day. **Avoid mode** (`Tracker.avoid`, success rule `clean`): you log slips, a day with nothing logged is a good day. The card (`screens/AvoidCard.tsx`) leads with the clean streak in the done colour ("12 days clean", "Today: clean so far", a best streak); "I slipped" is a small outlined button, a slip turns the card to the warning colour and opens an optional "What set it off?" note saved on that slip. **Daily** (`Tracker.daily`): saving again replaces today's reading.

Setup stays short and speed matters more than control. People start from a ready template, change little, and shape the rest on the tracker page itself (inline row editing, the Table wording). A tile-by-tile grid builder was tried and dropped as too much setup (see the case study; the code is archived in docs/archive/tile-builder).

## Plan editor

No edit mode. On the tracker page each row of a planned tracker has an edit button that opens that row's editor in place (`screens/PlanEditor.tsx`); one row is open at a time. The editor: name, number of repeats, a free-text target, move up or down (the only thing that affects other rows) and remove. Tick-list items edit name, group and the numbers they carry. Wording is per tracker (`Tracker.terms`: what an entry, a repeat and the target are called; neutral defaults Item, Round, Target; set in setup under Logging, "What it calls things") so the same template serves lifting, language practice, rehab and so on; it travels with a shared structure. Adding is a slim dashed "Add exercise" button at the end of the list that opens a small card (closed again with Done; an empty day opens it at once): a name, then sets and target. There is no list of suggestions from other days and no copy-a-whole-day (removed 2026-11-19: clutter, adding is quick, and "same list every day" in setup covers repeats). The day name sits at the top of the day. There are no rest days and no plan options on the page: whether one list is shared lives only in setup (Days), and which days a list-per-day tracker runs is just which days have lists. A day with no list is quiet: "Nothing on Tuesday" and a slim Add exercise button (the open "add your first" card shows only while the whole plan is empty); a day where you already logged another list still shows that list. There is no pick-another-list card (removed 2026-11-19: the same idea as copying a day). Plan trackers can look ahead in the week strip so any weekday's list is reachable; logging stays locked for future days. Plan edits apply from today and past days keep what they had, with one exception: a past day that had nothing planned (or was planned by a plan made today) can be built on. It is viewed through today's plan, what you add there applies from today on, and a note on the empty day says so. A past day that had a list is history and stays read-only for the plan. Entering values on past days is a separate rule: it follows Lock past days. Not yet: item alternatives, per-repeat targets.

## Schedules (stage 1 built 2026-11-17)

- Every tracker has a schedule (`core/schedule.ts`): every day, certain weekdays, every N days from a date, N times a week or month, once a month (date or last day), or whenever; plus an optional start/end window. `expectedOn` consults it, so skipped days are neither misses nor successes. Starting points set their own (Full body Mon/Wed/Fri; a Gym split's Mon/Wed/Fri is ignored while it has a list per day, see the next point), Running 3 a week, Spending, Symptoms and Savings whenever, the rest daily. Trackers saved earlier derive theirs from their plan.
- "N times a week or month" is stored as one goal (id `sched`), not two things.
- Lists decide the days (decided and built 2026-11-19): for a planned tracker with its own list on each weekday (not one shared list, not blocks), `scheduleOf` ignores the stored days and uses the weekdays that have something on them, per plan version. A weekday with something on it is a day to do it; an empty one has nothing to do and never counts against you. No rest-day flag. Adding to an empty weekday makes it a repeating day; emptying a day frees it. Everything without per-day lists (shared list, blocks, every few days, times, monthly, habits on certain days) keeps its stored schedule and day picker.
- Days (on the summary, no separate screen): a How often dropdown (Every day, Certain days, A few times a week, Every few days, Once a month, Whenever; a tracker that already has Blocks of days keeps that option) with the controls for the chosen one directly under it: the seven day toggles for certain days (starting with a few on, so on and off read clearly), a count for a number of times, and so on. The dropdown shows the day setting up front and signals that other rhythms exist. The card carries no explanatory subtext: the headlines carry the meaning. A planned tracker adds a Lists dropdown (The same list, A list per weekday, Choose each time with a count of lists); lists and days are named in the tracker itself, never in setup, and the two are linked: taking turns needs a number of times or whenever; leaving it for every day or certain days switches the lists back. Only for a while is a switch at the bottom of the card. A plan with a list per day runs on the weekdays chosen (any other day says it is not one of the tracker's days and points to setup).

## Reference: Nick's own Fitness Tracker (what "granular control" means)

Source: `C:\Users\nicho\Documents\Fitness Tracker\index.html`, a single-file web app, plan-driven. Capabilities that the prototype cannot express yet:
- **Plan versus free logging.** A weekly plan defines what to do (per weekday: focus, exercises, N sets, rep range such as 6-8 or 30-60s hold). Logging fills or ticks slots that already exist. A set counts only when ticked done, separately from typing weight and reps.
- **Checklist items that carry numbers.** Meals grouped by time, each food with protein and kcal, ticked to log; some foods have variants (chicken or beef) with different macros. Totals run against daily targets with progress bars.
- **Previous values inline.** Each set shows (as faint text in its boxes) the last logged weight and reps for the same exercise and set number from the most recent earlier day it was logged, whatever the weekday (so doing another day's list still finds its numbers), and ticking done auto-fills from it.
- **Plan versioning.** Each import is saved as a snapshot with an effective date, so past days show the plan that was active then.
- **Multi-day measures.** Morning weight with a 3-day rolling average.
- **No rest days (dropped 2026-11-18).** A day not in the schedule has no expectation and nothing is called rest; old trackers lose the flag and the "Rest" label on load.
- **Day navigation.** A week strip with dots per tracker, a calendar to browse history, and past days read-only.
- **Text export and import.** Export logs plus previous-week comparison plus the plan as plain text, paste into Claude to review, import an updated plan. History is preserved on import. This is already a "bring your own AI" loop with no API cost, and a candidate for the sharing format.

Implications: the model needs a plan layer (slots with targets, by weekday, versioned), previous-value lookup, measures that span days, rest days, and a text round trip. The key axis is planned slots versus free entries; most trackers will want one or the other, some both.

## Sharing a tracker's structure (fixed 2026-11-19)
A shared structure now carries everything that shapes the tracker: fields, measures, targets, success rule, tools including Countdown and Timer, wording, item style, the days or rhythm (and any start/end window), whether one list is shared, and the plan's lists. Older shared text without those still imports (they default). A schedule that is not well formed is rejected with a message instead of silently dropped. `presetSweep.test.ts` checks that every starting point survives a share round trip and a no-change save in setup, and that starting points have distinct descriptions.
- **Choices shaped in the entry**: on a form tracker, a Choice field can be changed where you log it: a + chip adds an option (and picks it); an Edit button (or press and hold an option) shows × on each so they can be removed. No "pick any" label. Mood starts with an empty list, so the list is the person's own. A text field marked `optional` shows as a small "+ Note" until wanted (one-off words, never added to a list). A button that has done its job (Saved) is not dimmed: it turns outlined in the done colour with a tick (`Button done`). Done uses the person's highlight colour exactly (no separate green, no hue shift): the treatment (outlined, a dot, a tick) is what says done. A one-a-day form tracker shows its rolling average as a line under the value it averages, not in a summary card.
- **Typed decimals in the stepper**: up to 6 whole digits and 2 decimals (`cleanDecimal`); a keystroke past that is refused and the box keeps what it had, so the control never outgrows its row and nothing flashes and undoes itself. The + stays at the right edge, so growth is toward the −. A one-a-day average line is always shown under its value, with "—" until there is data, so nothing shifts when the first entry is saved.
- **Units per tracker, on the summary**: any tracker with unit-bearing numbers (weight, distance, length, volume) shows a Units card on its setup summary: the units in use, and chips for My default, Imperial and Metric that switch this tracker alone (goal numbers convert with it, via `changeUnits`). Values are stored in base units, so switching only changes the display.
- **Step size and starting value in the builder**: a number's editor has "Plus and minus move by", so a from-scratch Steps tracker can step by 500. There is no starting-value control: a starting value is the one thing a preset has over a custom builder. Presets keep their own starting values (a blood pressure stepping from 0 by 1 would be unusable on day one); after the first entry, "previous numbers" takes over.
- **The bottom row keeps its place**: its scroll position is remembered (for the session) and restored whenever the bar is rebuilt, such as coming back from a tracker's setup. It moves only when a tracker is added (to the new one) or, the first time, to bring the current tracker into view.
- **Timer fills any minutes field**: the timer's "Put the time in" can name an hours-and-minutes field or a plain number measured in min (`canTimerFill`). The time counted is put in the form unsaved. A field you changed by hand is never replaced: the timer's time is offered ("Timer: 25 min", Use it, Ignore). Reading is an hours-and-minutes field with a timer into it, and its step is set per field ("Plus and minus move by", in minutes for a duration).
- **Editing a logged entry** (every tracker that lists entries): numbers and times (distance, minutes, a single number) sit in the row as the controls used to enter them, with a small × to remove; an entry with words, a choice or a rating shows its summary and opens in place to the same controls. A change is the entry's new value at once, with nothing to confirm (it is already a record). The form at the top is only for new entries; there is no Edit button or edit mode. Each page starts at the top: a setup step or another tracker resets the scroll.
- **Bottom row**: it has a gap under it (`NAV.gap`) on top of the phone's inset, and scrolls with normal momentum, settling on an item, instead of a hard snap.
- **Time amounts are hours and minutes**: the Time chip and the Running, Study time, Practice, Workout log, Time log and Reading presets use the hours-and-minutes field with a timer into it. Older trackers of those presets move on load (`upgradeReadingMinutes`, `MINUTES_PRESETS`). In logged rows, a field's name is shown only when its unit would not say which is which (distance in mi and time in h min need no names; three mmHg numbers do); rows are separated by a subtle line. The bottom row scrolls freely with momentum, no snapping. The delete confirmation is one line with its note's space always reserved.
- **Stacked numbers and the stepper**: the number is left-aligned in its box (the whole digits never move when a decimal appears; the box widens only when a whole digit is gained). When several numbers or durations are stacked, in the form or in a logged row, they share one total width (`fit`) so every − and + lines up, with the number and unit at the left of the space between (never centred, so nothing recentres as you type). Logged rows have a line between entries, none under the last.
- **One frame for number boxes**: steppers, hours-and-minutes and time-of-day all sit in `StepFrame` (minus, value, plus; `fit` for a shared width) and take their measurements from `NUMBER_BOX` in constants/layout.ts (digit and point width, padding, unit gap, gap between hours and minutes). A stepper reserves room for a decimal only when it can have one and never more than the two it can hold. Hours and minutes right-align their digits against their units.
- **Number box rule (supersedes the shared-width idea)**: no shared width. Every number box is only as wide as its digits and sits against its + button, so the − stays the same short distance from the first digit and more digits move the box left. Hours and minutes work the same way: a one-digit minute is narrower than a two-digit one and the hours shift with it. A stepper reserves room for the decimals its step can produce (0.5 gives one, 0.25 two, a whole step none) from the start, so no press moves the number. Stacked controls line up at the + only.
- **Number box, current rule**: every typed number (stepper and each of hours and minutes) is `NumberBox`: plain text until tapped, then a text box made for that one edit and gone after it, so the minus and plus never touch a text box. A box is as wide as its digits (a stepper also keeps room for the decimals its step can produce). The frame (`StepFrame`) puts the same space (`NUMBER_BOX.edge`) between the minus and the first digit as between the last character or unit and the plus. Boxes stacked in a form or a logged row sit in a `StepGroup`: the widest decides the frame width, so every minus lines up in one place and every plus in another; a narrower box keeps its number against the minus and the spare space falls before its plus. This replaces the earlier "stacked controls line up at the plus only" and fixed-width ideas.
- **Number box, final**: the phone's own select-all is not used anywhere (it kept re-triggering). The number opens highlighted by our own drawing (accent fill, inverted text, 2 px either side, 18 px tall, no caret) and the first key replaces it (`firstKey`); the real box underneath is hidden with view transparency until then. The first tap after the highlight places a caret without the phone's drag handle; a second tap gives the normal one. That is accepted as normal Android behaviour.
- Header: no "Today · done" line. Number boxes are never narrower than their step's digits; a box follows its digits live: growing is instant, shrinking eases (faster the more digits go at once). Timer stop always fills its field (no offer).
- Number boxes ease to a new width (140 ms) in the shared NumberBox.
- Switching places: the new page slides in (56 px + fade, 200 ms) from the side it sits on the bar. Not on first launch.

- First run: back from the first tracker returns to the name and look (revisit); no header bar on the first start step. Keyboard close glides the page back before removing the reserved space.

- Safe areas: `useTopPad` (kit) pads by the phone's reported insets, never less than the fixed guess; screens without the tracker bar get a card-coloured strip under them for the phone navigation. Look previews resolve the real theme for each option.

- Number boxes: digits right-aligned (whole steps), control right-anchored, growth snaps; losing a digit or so snaps too, and a bigger drop eases. Logged entries are editable in place: numbers, times, ratings and a name are the row itself; choices and notes sit on one line beneath it (their values, or their names when empty) that opens to the same controls. Anything that opens in place scrolls into view (useReveal).

**Opening choice, timers (current).** In "The one I need" and "Where I was", a running stopwatch or countdown (or a countdown that ended in the last half hour) decides which tracker opens, even if that tracker is done or not due today. With several, the one started last wins. A stopwatch left running over 12 hours is treated as forgotten. "Always the same one" ignores timers. This applies when the app opens fresh, not when it comes back from the background.

**Day picker (current).** A tracker that runs every day shows the words "Every day" on the summary, with no row of seven filled days; open Days to pick certain days. Once only some days are on, the row shows on the summary.

**Opening choice, safeguards (current).** Among trackers still waiting, ones with nothing logged for 14 days (and not newly made) go to the end of the line, so an abandoned tracker cannot become the default. The usual time comes from days of the same kind as today (weekday or weekend) when there are at least 5 of them, else from all days; days logged afterwards by hand (stamped noon) are ignored. A quick reopen goes back to where you were unless that tracker is finished and others are waiting. The behaviour is checked over 60 simulated days for six kinds of routine in startPickSim.test.ts.

**Pace and GPX (current).** A pace (time over distance) reads as minutes and seconds per distance unit ("8:30 /mi"), not a decimal. `core/gpx.ts` reads a GPX file into distance, elapsed and moving time, elevation gain and loss (changes under 3 m ignored), a route simplified to about 300 points, and splits per mile or kilometre. It is arithmetic only; nothing uses it on screen yet (the import screen and where a run's route and splits are kept are still to be designed).

**Live timers in the notification area (current, Android development build).** Starting a stopwatch or a countdown shows an ongoing notification with the tracker's name and a clock the system ticks itself (counting up for a stopwatch, down for a countdown), so it keeps going with the app closed or the phone locked. Pausing or resetting removes it; a countdown's notification goes away when it runs out, and the existing "Countdown finished" alert speaks. A resumed stopwatch carries on from the time already counted. It does nothing on the web, in Expo Go or with notifications refused; the timer itself is unaffected. Pure payload in `core/liveSpec.ts` (tested), display in `core/liveTimer.ts`. Tapping it opens the app, which opens on the timed tracker.

**Finished countdown alert (current).** The "Time's up" alert is an exact alarm that rings until dismissed. It is cleared as soon as you are in the app (on opening or returning to it, or when a countdown runs out while you are looking at it), and the app's own buzz happens only if the countdown ran out within the last couple of seconds, so returning to the app later never buzzes again. Handing countdowns to the phone's Clock app was tried and dropped: Samsung's Clock shows the status-bar chip, but it ignores cancel requests from other apps, so pause and reset could not stay in step.

**Notifications open their own tracker (current).** Tapping a timer notification or a finished-countdown alert opens the tracker it belongs to, whatever the opening rule would have chosen; opening the app from its icon still uses the rule. The notification text names the tracker and, for a countdown, how long it was set for ("Gym split", "2 min countdown"; the alert reads "Gym split · 2 min"). **Reset on the timer** keeps what was counted: when the timer fills a field, Reset puts the time into it first (as Pause does), unless under a minute, so reaching for Reset never loses a value; the field is still not saved until you save.

**Take turns (rotation) (current).** For people who train on irregular days. A planned tracker can have lists that are not tied to weekdays (Push, Pull, Legs; two to seven, named in setup). Today's page shows them as chips with the suggested one marked "next": the list after the one last trained, wrapping round, so skipped days never move it. One tap picks the list for the day; after logging, the day shows the list you logged, and tapping another starts a second. The schedule can only be a number of times or whenever, so nothing reads as missed. Stored as a plan version flagged `rotation` whose `days` are just the lists; changing layout keeps built lists by position. It is reached from the Lists row of a planned tracker (the Gym split preset is the usual starting point; the weekday layout is its default and its days are editable in the Days screen).

**Speed (current).** The tracker screen no longer recalculates the game and progress picture on every change (it only needed the plain state); that recalculation took tens of milliseconds on a desktop with a year of data. The dev build runs in development mode, which is slower than a finished app.

- Steps tracker: a card fills the day's steps from Health Connect (Android), with states for not installed, not allowed, allowed and no data. Permission is the standard Android allow screen; reads steps only.

- Steps card: while on "phone", the day's value follows Health Connect (asked every 20 s while the page is open and on returning to the app). A chip on the card switches between "Use my phone" and "Enter myself" (stored in Tracker.sources by field id); manual is never overwritten.

- Phone steps: on app start and each return to the front, the last 7 days of every tracker set to "phone" are filled from Health Connect (the app cannot read while closed; Health Connect holds the days meanwhile).

- Steps: one switch, "Fill in automatically" (on by default). Behind it the phone's own counter (saved every 15 min in the background, read every 5 s while open), (Health Connect only on a phone with no step counter). While automatic the field follows the count; typing in it or using plus/minus turns automatic off. Native module: `app/modules/trackbench-steps`.
